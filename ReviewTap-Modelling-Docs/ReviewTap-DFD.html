Customer Review Journey — Scan to Google Review

The customer scans the business QR (web address like …/biz/cafe-name) or a staff member's personal QR.

Step 1 · Visit is recordedThe system notes which business (and staff member) the scan belongs to, and whether the customer uses a phone or computer
▼if they scanned a staff QR…
Step 2 · Rate the staff memberCustomer gives 1–5 stars for behaviour, speed and overall service
▼
Step 3 · Branded welcome pageShows the business logo, colors and staff name, with 3 big buttons
▼
Button 1 · Direct Google ReviewOpens Google directly so the customer can post (this path is not tracked)
Button 2 · AI Writing HelpCustomer answers 2–3 quick questions → the AI drafts a review → customer edits it → presses “Copy & Continue” → the review is pasted on Google → thank-you message. The draft and the Google click are saved.
Button 3 · Private FeedbackCustomer writes a private message for the management → saved as unread → thank-you message

Table Complaint Journey — Scan to Private Complaint

Each table has its own QR. Scanning it opens a page like …/complaint/abc123 — that secret code already tells the system the business and the table, so printed QRs never need reprinting.

Owner setup (done once)Add tables (with branch name if any) → print one QR per table → customize the complaint options and the page heading. Ready-made options are filled in automatically: Slow Service, Staff Behaviour, Food Quality, Price, Cleanliness, Other.
▼customer scans the table QR (no login, no typing of table number)
The link is checkedThe system finds the right business and table. A switched-off QR shows “This QR code is currently unavailable”; a wrong link shows “Invalid Link”.
▼
Complaint formBusiness logo, colors and table name on top → “What went wrong?” with tappable options (more than one can be picked) → optional description box → optional name → Submit Complaint
▼
Complaint is saved privatelyStatus starts as “new” · a copy of the table and option names is kept so history stays correct even if the owner renames things later · it never becomes a Google review → “Thank you for letting us know.”
▼
Owner handles it in the inboxFilter by new / in progress / resolved / closed · see full details (ID, branch, table, options, date and time) · change status · write private notes that customers can never see

House Rules — Things That Apply Everywhere

How the website stays safe, fresh and easy to use on all pages.

  • Everyone sees only their own business: owners log in securely, and every list is filtered to their business — one café can never see another café's complaints or data.
  • Pages refresh themselves: if nobody touches the screen for 30 seconds, the page quietly reloads its data (anything you already typed stays as it is).
  • Buttons show they are working: every Save/Update/Delete button spins and disables itself while the work is being done, so nobody taps twice by mistake.
  • Works on phones too: the 3-dot button at the top-left opens the side menu with all sections; computer screens keep the normal sidebar.
  • QR code web addresses: printed QRs always contain the main website address; the on-screen Preview buttons use short links so testing on a laptop works too.
  • Business photos live in photo storage: logos and cover images are shrunk automatically and kept in Firebase photo storage, then shown on the customer pages.
  • Long lists scroll in place: tables, QR grids and option lists scroll inside their own boxes instead of making the whole page endless.
